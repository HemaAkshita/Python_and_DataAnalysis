# Summary of Exploratory Data Analysis

The dataset analyzed contains information related to various health indicators and risk factors associated with cardiovascular conditions. The features include age, BMI, hypertension status, average glucose level, smoking status, residence type, and heart disease occurrence. Through the analysis, I gained a better understanding of how demographic and lifestyle factors may influence the likelihood of developing heart disease and hypertension.

One of the main challenges faced during the analysis was selecting appropriate visualizations for different types of variables. Since the dataset contained both categorical and numerical features, it was necessary to choose different plotting techniques such as bar charts, boxplots, histograms, and scatter plots. Another challenge was interpreting correlation values. Initially, some correlations appeared weaker than expected, but further investigation revealed that medical datasets often contain multiple interacting factors, making strong linear correlations uncommon. Handling missing values and understanding the presence of outliers, particularly in the BMI feature, also required additional attention.

Several visualizations proved particularly useful during the analysis. Histograms helped in understanding the distribution of numerical variables such as age and BMI. Boxplots were effective for detecting outliers and analyzing data spread. Bar charts were useful for comparing heart disease prevalence across categories such as smoking status and residence type. Correlation matrices and heatmaps helped identify relationships between numerical variables and provided a quick overview of feature interactions.

The analysis revealed several important insights. Age was found to be one of the strongest factors associated with both hypertension and heart disease. Heart disease was more prevalent among individuals above 40 years of age, while hypertension was primarily observed in adults above 20 years. Former smokers showed the highest prevalence of heart disease, followed by current smokers, whereas non-smokers exhibited the lowest prevalence. Rural and urban populations showed similar rates of hypertension and heart disease, with only slight differences between them. Additionally, the dataset contained BMI outliers, indicating the presence of individuals with unusually high or low body mass indices.

This project provided valuable experience in Exploratory Data Analysis (EDA). I learned how to inspect data quality, identify missing values and outliers, analyze feature distributions, study relationships between variables, and extract meaningful insights from visualizations. Most importantly, I learned that EDA is not just about creating plots but about understanding the story hidden within the data and using evidence to support conclusions. These skills form a strong foundation for future machine learning and data science projects.
